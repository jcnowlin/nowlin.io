## Defining Gateway Knowledge

Gateway knowledge, as described in the ALA Core Competences, are the minimum skills development provided by ALA accredited graduate programs necessary for entry-level librarianship. 11 skills are identified as essential, which includes foundational professional principles, democratic/intellectual freedom principles, historical societal role and evolution of that role, understand various types of information organizations, significant policies and trends impacting information organizations, laws regulating libraries from all levels of government and various adjacent laws, effective advocacy for all engaged with the library as well as services, techniques to identify appropriate and collaborative solutions to complex problems information organizations face, demonstrate effective verbal and written communication in a wide range of options, and hold current credentials for specialized segments of librarianship.

## Competency Fulfillment

The foundation for my fulfilment of the competency Gateway Knowledge is based in coursework from INFO 5000: Information & Knowledge Professions, which included assignments that introduced and reinforced topics like intellectual freedom, privacy, diversity, and legal/ethical concepts that impact the profession. Other INFO 5000 assignments enforced knowledge in topics the historical and evolving societal role of information organizations. Other courses, like INFO 5320: Public Libraries, delved deeper into the history of public libraries, my current domain, and gave me the full background and current role public libraries in communities. INFO 5320 helped me to intimately understand how my current employer's, Garland County Library, history and background.

Exercises in INFO 5320, INFO 5300: Management of Information Agencies and INFO 5302: Advanced Management of Information Agencies enforced a deep dive in collaboration with others to analyze topics like societal policies and trends, the legal framework dictating library operations as well as laws and principles dictating individual rights, which ultimately also provided me with practical experience collaboration with others. I, most importantly, gained an understanding of the effects of both effective and ineffective advocacy for libraries, staff, and services, not to mention effective oppositional advocacy designed to harm libraries, workers, and services.

Due to my scholarship in INFO 5000, 5300, 5302, and 5320, as well as preceding knowledge, I am knowledgeable of various different types of information organizations, the dissemination of information and its background, and development of the skills to successfully draft appropriate and collaborative solutions to identified problems after analyzation. The gateway knowledge I have earned has already been deployed in my day job, on administrative boards I have participated in, and committees like the Association of Bookmobile and Outreach Services Bylaws Committee. Additionally, I used my knowledge of the First Amendment and derived freedoms like Freedom of Expression and Intellectual Freedom, most recently by tying in my deceased uncle's love of First Amendment Auditor videos to First Amendment principles and the importance of the First Amendment to democracy (before handing the service over to the pastor).

## Exemplification Assignment

My term paper submitted for INFO 5000 titled *Pressures Impacting Public Libraries*, includes discussion about far-right political influence on libraries and Intellectual Freedom in Sweden, the importance of library advocacy by the failure and rebirth of a public library in Oregon, the impact of the 2008 economy had on public libraries and how libraries adapted, the 2017 threat of eliminating IMLS funding to libraries and possible consequences, balancing information dissemination services between physical and digital resources while accounting for costs vs. accessibility, a review of challenges in public libraries in Denmark, as well as the role of public libraries as social spaces and embracing it. Furthermore, my submission also included discussion on censorship pressures from all levels of government entities and combating it, considerations for selection and reconsideration policies and improving them, an appointment of the harshly antagonistic Arkansas State Library Board Member Jason Rapert and his rejection of Intellectual Freedom, who has now been removed from the board in a full-reset of the ASL board during the Summer of 2025.

This submission, which concluded with advocacy and driving value as the topics most impacting public libraries, highlights each concept required for sufficient gateway knowledge, some more than others.
